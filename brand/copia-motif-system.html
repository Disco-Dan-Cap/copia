01 — The Motif

One shape, tiled with intention. That's the whole motif system.

Copia doesn't get a separate decorative pattern, a separate texture, and a separate brand wallpaper. It gets one motif — the Leaf Wave — built from the same leaf-mark paths that carry every other tier of the logo system. Three densities. A handful of color pairings. Strict rules for when it shows up.

Leaf Wave · Dense · Deepest Forest on Mint
02 — Density

Three intensities. Each one has a job.

The same motif at three densities, each tuned for a different surface. Dense for hero canvases that earn the busy. Medium for marketing surfaces where the pattern is the room. Sparse for in-product contexts where the pattern is the wallpaper, not the message.

07 — Rules

The principles behind the motif.

One motif, three densities. No fourth.

The system is intentionally small: dense for hero, medium for marketing surfaces, sparse for in-product backdrops. Resist the urge to add a fourth density — the more variants, the less recognizable the pattern.

The motif is a backdrop, never a subject.

The pattern's job is to give product and message a place to live. The product, the wordmark, the headline always sit on top. If the motif is competing for attention with the content, it's at the wrong density — step it down.

Dense + busy content = forbidden.

The dense pattern only works behind quiet content (the wordmark, a single big headline, a logo lockup). If the surface has cards, lists, or paragraphs, drop to medium or sparse, or knock the pattern out entirely beneath the content block.

Sparse is the in-product default.

For empty states, splash screens inside the app, and any product surface that wants warmth without busy-ness, use leafWave-sparse at opacity: 0.5 or lower. The pattern should feel like grain, not wallpaper.

Single-color only.

The motif is one color of leaf on one color of background. No multi-tone leaves, no gradients inside the pattern, no patterned-leaves-on-patterned-background. The leaf mark is single-color by rule; the pattern inherits that rule.

Body text needs a panel.

Headlines and the wordmark can sit directly on any density. Body type cannot — the negative spaces in the leaves create reading friction. If body type lives on a motif surface, give it a cream card with a 16 px+ corner radius.
