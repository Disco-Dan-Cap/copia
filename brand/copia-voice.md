# Copia Voice

How Copia sounds, in product and on the page. The companion skill `copia-frontend-design` governs what Copia looks like; this one governs what it says. The two share a method: define the register positively, then ban the generic defaults outright.

## The register

Warm, confident, slightly knowing. Never preachy, never crunchy. Short sentences for hero copy; fuller sentences for explanation. It should read like a thoughtful editor wrote it — not a marketing team, and not a language model.

The product's own copy is the benchmark. These shipped lines are the voice:

- *"Nothing in your basket yet. The market's open."* (empty state — calm, a touch of welcome)
- *"You'll collect the heirloom tomatoes and backyard eggs from Mira at the Mueller market Saturday morning."* (logistics as correspondence — names, places, days; never "the grower," never "your order")
- *"It's arranged."* (a confirmation headline — two words where the genre yells)
- *"Mira, that's a real question and the honest answer is: not yet, and not this week."* (the Coach declining to flatter — honesty as warmth)
- *"Not now."* (a dismiss control — invitational register, never an X-toast)

Notice what these do: they name people and places, they state things plainly, and they trust the reader. Write toward that.

## Case-study & long-form prose rules

The case study is written in Garrison's first person. He is a designer and founder describing his own decisions — direct, specific, willing to admit a mistake, never grandiose about it.

1. **Specifics are the authenticity.** Real names (Mira, the Mueller market), real numbers ($1M–$1.5M, 47 interviews, 16s → 0.26s), real dates, verbatim quotes from the 2021 archives. An abstract claim that could appear in anyone's portfolio is a wasted sentence.
2. **Computed, not estimated.** If a number can be calculated, calculate it and print the real value. If it can't be verified, either mark its provenance ("as we sized it in 2021") or cut it.
3. **One quiet flourish per section, maximum.** A single aphoristic close ("The decision made itself.") can land. Two in a row is a poetry reading. When in doubt, end on a plain statement of fact — calm prose demonstrates the brand's maturity better than announcing it. (This rule exists because the first rename-section draft ended "the original was loved, and wrong" — Garrison cut it for being dramatic. He was right.)
4. **Admissions earn trust.** "I tried to talk myself into the month grid once. The brief won." First-person concessions, dead ends, and declined temptations make the wins credible. Include them.
5. **Rules ship with reasons.** Never state a design rule without its rationale in the same breath — "the calendar is bounded at four weeks *because nobody buys squash a month out*."
6. **Italics are rare and load-bearing.** One italic phrase when it earns emphasis. Never italicize body text decoratively. Never two flourishes (italic + aphorism) in the same sentence.

## The ban list (the "sounds like AI" tells)

These patterns are banned because they are the statistical signature of generated prose. Each one is individually defensible; in aggregate they are a watermark. The goal is prose a skeptical design director reads without once thinking about how it was produced.

**Constructions:**
- "It's not just X — it's Y." / "not only… but also" — the contrast-reveal reflex. State the thing directly.
- Rule-of-three everywhere. Copia's own hero ends on a triplet ("calm, personal, unhurried") — that's allowed *because it's rare*. If triplets appear in consecutive paragraphs, cut all but the best one.
- Stacked parallel sentence openers ("It sells… It is… Its money…") — fine once as a deliberate device, never as a default rhythm.
- The summary-restate close ("In short, …", "Ultimately, …", "At its core, …"). If the section needs summarizing, it's too long.
- Rhetorical-question transitions ("So what does this mean?").

**Vocabulary (banned in Copia prose):** delve, leverage (verb), robust, seamless, vibrant, foster, empower, testament, tapestry, landscape (figurative), journey (except "customer journey" as a term of art), elevate, game-changing, revolutionize, cutting-edge, "best experience," utilize, holistic, synergy, "truly," "incredibly," "deeply" as intensifiers.

**Punctuation & typography:**
- Em-dashes: useful, house-sanctioned, and the single most recognizable AI tell when overused. Maximum two per paragraph; if a draft paragraph has three, rewrite one clause as its own sentence.
- No exclamation points anywhere in Copia prose. (The 2021 wireframe's "150 sellers in your area!" is quoted in the case study precisely as the *before* picture.)
- No emoji, ever. No ✨ especially.
- Bold sparingly — for the one load-bearing term in a section, not for emphasis-sprinkling.

**Rhythm:**
- Vary sentence length on purpose. A long sentence that walks through a decision, then a short one. Like this.
- Vary paragraph length too. Uniform four-sentence paragraphs read as generated even when each is individually fine.
- Don't open consecutive paragraphs with the same word or construction.

## Self-check before finishing any prose

Read the draft once asking only these questions:
1. Could any sentence appear in another company's case study unchanged? Rewrite it with a specific.
2. Count the em-dashes and triplets. Over budget? Cut.
3. Does more than one section end on an aphorism? Demote the weaker ones to plain statements.
4. Is every number either computed or provenance-marked?
5. Read the first clause of every paragraph in sequence — do they vary?
6. Does it contain any ban-list word? (Search, don't trust memory.)

## Before / after exemplars

**Dramatic → calm (real revision from the case study):**
- Before: *"Renaming your own founding idea is a strange kind of design work — a self-critique with a deliverable. It was the right call in the way the hardest design calls usually are: the original was loved, and wrong."*
- After: *"The old name had five years of sentiment behind it; the new one did more work. The decision made itself."*

**Generic → specific:**
- Before: *"The checkout was redesigned to be simpler and more user-friendly."*
- After: *"Instead of the five-step wizard, one calm page — and the plan is narrated back as a sentence: 'You'll collect the heirloom tomatoes from Mira at the Mueller market Saturday morning.'"*

**AI-cadence → human cadence:**
- Before: *"This isn't just a feature — it's a philosophy. It empowers growers, elevates the buying experience, and fosters genuine community."*
- After: *"The Coach exists so a person with a yard and a notion can become a seller. That's the whole reason."*
