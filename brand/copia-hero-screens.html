The memorable moment

The masthead reading "Peaches are early this year." A buyer screenshots that. A buyer texts that to a friend. It establishes from the first second that Copia knows what's actually growing, where, and when — and writes about it like a thoughtful editor, not a marketing team.

What we explicitly didn't do

No SaaS hero formula. No purple gradient. No "Sign up for 20% off" pop-up. No trust-badge row. No metric tiles. No emoji icons. The "Start growing to sell" CTA is one editorial banner near the bottom of the scroll, not an interruption.

The system, applied

Cream canvas. Forest carries voice; charcoal carries body; terracotta appears once as the "2 new listings" pulse dot. Leaf Wave sparse motif sits behind the masthead at 50% opacity, doing texture work without competing. The wordmark in nav uses the simplified flat treatment per the leaf-mark spec.

Why iPhone first

Discovery happens on a phone — at a desk Wednesday afternoon, in line at HEB, in bed Sunday morning. Copia is an iPhone-first PWA: 390 px logical width baseline, safe-area-aware (the tab bar sits above the home-indicator gutter you see floating at the bottom of this frame), installable to the home screen via Safari's share sheet. The mint app-icon tile lives on the home screen as a Copia brand moment, not a generic web shortcut.

The memorable moment

The Coach Card. An AI insight written like a knowledgeable friend's note, not a notification. Specific demand signal, specific harvest window, specific price recommendation with reasoning — then a single primary action ("Draft Thursday's listing"). One card. Earns its placement.

The thing it refuses to be

A dashboard. There is no row of metric cards with up-arrows and percentages. The three "today" figures are set as editorial figures with explanatory subtext — a magazine page about Maya's Tuesday, not an analytics screen.

Subtle craft

The sidebar runs on deepest forest with the Leaf Wave motif at 8% opacity — present but not loud. The active state uses mint as the tint and a 3px mint border on the left, never solid mint background. The "Three" in pickups uses the Fraunces italic — one small typographic move that signals editorial, not enterprise.

Why desktop for this one

Sellers do their planning at a table. The calendar strip, the listings grid, and the Coach Card all want horizontal breathing room. A mobile version exists (collapsed sidebar, vertically stacked figures, swipeable calendar), but the dashboard is most itself on a screen wider than your hand.

The memorable moment

The whole composition reading like a personal letter. The salute "Maya —", the seven-day strip on a deep-forest panel with color-coded action tags, the closing "Coach's consider" card written as a friend's nudge. There is no chat thread anywhere on this screen.

Why not a chat

Every AI feature ships as a chat thread. That's the default trap. Copia's Growing Coach isn't a Q&A assistant — it's a weekly publication for one. The chat input is still there at the bottom (you can ask "when should I prune my tomatoes?") but it's a tertiary affordance, not the experience.

The strategic role

This screen carries the buyer-to-seller conversion thesis. Without the Growing Coach, the seller cold-start is impossible — anyone with a balcony is intimidated. With it, anyone with a balcony has a friend who tells them exactly what to plant, when to harvest, what to list. The case study points here.

Type & color choices

Fraunces italic for "lettuce" and "chefs" — the only place we use a serif, and only for emphasis the brand wants the reader to feel. The seven-day strip inverts to deepest-forest with mint accents, which gives the page a strong second register and lets the action tags (harvest, plant, water, list, rest) read as color-coded chips without crowding the cream above.

04 — Stage 2 · Closed

The brand works at screen scale. Stage 3 begins.

Five brand artifacts plus three hero screens. Neo-Natural holds up not just on a palette card but in a real product context, on real devices, with real content. The system is ready to scale into the full app build.

What just shipped

Buyer Home as an editorial first impression. Seller Dashboard as a letter, not a control panel. AI Growing Coach as a weekly composition, not a chatbot. Three distinct contexts, one coherent brand voice. The diagnostic questions from the design directive (1972 co-op? 2026 studio? memorable moment?) all answer yes.
