01 — The Mark

One symbol carries the whole brand. Treat it carefully.

The Copia leaf mark is the brand's smallest expression and its hardest-working asset. It is the favicon, the app icon, the watermark, the loading state, the chip on a button. These are the rules that keep it recognizable at 16 pixels and at a billboard.

02 — Variants

Four approved colorways. No others.

Each variant maps to a specific surface. Use the one that matches the background you're rendering on. The mark is single-color, always — no gradients, no shadows, no fills inside the negative space.

03 — Size

Built to read from 16 pixels to a billboard.

The mark is engineered to hold its silhouette at small sizes. Minimum size is 16×22 px digital, 8 mm print. Below that, the negative space between paths collapses and the mark stops reading as a leaf.

04 — Clear Space

Give the mark room to breathe.

Maintain a minimum of 0.35 × mark height of clear space on every side. No type, no other marks, no UI chrome inside this safe area. The dashed frame below shows the minimum.

The safe area equals 0.35 × the mark's height on all sides.
05 — App Icon

The icon is always centered on forest.

Across iOS, Android, watchOS, and web favicons, the app icon is the same: the leaf in mint, centered on a forest tile. Platform-specific corner radius is the only variable. Never use the cream-on-forest variant for the icon — cream blends with system chrome on light mode.

06 — Lockup

When the mark and wordmark appear together.

Use the wordmark alone in marketing and on the splash screen. Use the mark alone for the icon, watermark, and any small context. Use the horizontal lockup for app headers and email signatures. The stacked lockup is for square contexts only — profile photos, share cards, packaging.

08 — Rules

The principles behind the mechanics.

The mark is single-color, always.

No gradients, no inner shadows, no two-tone treatments. The wordmark can be gummy; the mark stays flat. This is how the system separates "brand moment" (wordmark) from "brand presence" (mark).

The orientation is fixed.

The mark is drawn to read at this specific orientation. Rotating it for "design interest" breaks the visual logic of the form — the paths only resolve into a leaf at this angle.

App icon is mint on forest, no exceptions.

Reads correctly on every OS theme (light, dark, system-tinted). The cream-on-forest variant fails on iOS large-icon contexts where iOS applies its own background tinting. Mint on forest is always safe.

Below 16 px, use a flat dot.

For 12 px and smaller contexts (system tray, ultra-dense lists), substitute a 4 px forest dot rather than a crushed mark. A dot says "Copia"; a crushed mark says "broken Copia".

Clear space is non-negotiable.

The 0.35 × height safe area applies even when space is tight. If the layout can't accommodate it, the mark is too big for that layout — scale it down, don't compress the breathing room.

The wordmark and the mark are not the same asset.

The wordmark says the name. The mark is the brand. Don't substitute one for the other — using the wordmark as a favicon (it will crush) or the mark on a marketing hero (it leaves the name unsaid) both fail.
