01 — Foundation

Seven greens, one cream, one black, one warm. Everything else is restraint.

The Copia color system isn't decorated onto the brand — it was extracted from the logo. The seven-step green ramp lives inside the gummy wordmark; the cream and the warm accent finish the system. Each color has a defined role, a contrast budget, and a rule for when it shows up.

03 — Neutrals & Accent

Cream is the canvas. Charcoal is the body. Terracotta is the spike.

Mint is for brand moments; cream is what the actual product is built on. Charcoal — not black — keeps body type warm. Terracotta is the only non-green color in the system and exists to do one job: pull your eye where the product needs it.

05 — Rules

Where each color is allowed.

A palette card without rules is just decoration. These are the constraints that keep the brand from drifting as the system scales.

Mint is for moments, not screens.

Retro mint (#9CE5D0) is the brand wash — splash, onboarding hero, marketing pages, packaging. The in-app product runs on cream, not mint. Mint at screen scale gets exhausting; mint at moment scale feels iconic.

Forest carries the brand voice.

Headlines, primary buttons, the wordmark itself: forest (#1C664D). Body type stays charcoal so the page reads warm, not green. The brand isn't about green — green is the wallpaper, not the message.

Sage shadow is structural, never typographic.

Sage shadow (#4E8472) lives in dividers, card borders, low-emphasis chrome. It does not carry text. Contrast against cream is 4.00:1 — passes AA large only, fails everywhere else.

Terracotta is the only spike.

One non-green color, one job: pull the eye to the thing that needs attention. Notifications, "new" badges, the urgent CTA. If terracotta appears more than once per screen, something's wrong with the screen.

Never pure black.

Charcoal (#1A1A1A) is the warmest believable body color. Pure #000 on cream reads as harsh and digital — opposite of the brand. Same rule applies to the SVG leaf mark: render in forest or sage, not black.

Light sage and sage are surface colors.

Use them for tags, info cards, soft fills, and seller-spectrum visualizations. They can hold large display type (sage + charcoal at 5.15:1 passes AA), but they're not the default surface — cream is.
